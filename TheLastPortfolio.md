# Silicon Maze: The Last Portfolio

## The Story

The **Doomsday event has begun**.

Systems are failing, networks are disappearing, and the world is running out of time.

Before everything goes offline, every survivor is given one final mission:

**Preserve your story.**

Your mission is to build **The Last Portfolio** — a personal portfolio website that captures who you are, what you can do, and what you have built.

The portfolio should feel like a **digital survival archive** from the world of Doomsday.

It should not feel like a collection of unrelated sections. Each part of the website should contribute to one cohesive experience that represents you.

The overall experience should follow a **Doomsday-themed interface** — futuristic, immersive, and creative.

---

# Task 1: The Survivor Profile

### Personal Introduction — 25 points

Before the archive can be accessed, it needs to identify its survivor.

Build the main section of your portfolio that introduces who you are.

### Subtask 1.1 — Identity (10 pts)

Create a clear introduction section containing:

- Your name.
- Your role / field of interest.
- A short introduction about yourself.
- Your areas of interest.

The introduction should immediately communicate who you are to someone visiting your portfolio for the first time.

### Subtask 1.2 — The Survivor's Log (15 pts)

Create an **About Me** section containing relevant information about yourself.

You may include:

- Education.
- Technical interests.
- Hobbies.
- Goals.
- Achievements.
- Clubs or communities.
- A short personal story.

The information should be presented creatively rather than as one large block of text.

---

# Task 2: The Arsenal

### Skills & Technologies — 20 points

Every survivor needs the right tools.

Create a section showcasing the technologies and skills you know or are currently learning.

The section may include:

- Programming languages.
- Frameworks.
- Developer tools.
- Databases.
- Design tools.
- Other relevant technical skills.

### Subtask 2.1 — Skill Archive (10 pts)

Organise your skills into clear categories.

For example:

- Languages.
- Frontend.
- Backend.
- Databases.
- Tools.

The exact categories are up to you.

Avoid presenting your skills as a simple comma-separated list.

### Subtask 2.2 — Arsenal Interface (10 pts)

Present your skills using a visually engaging interface.

You may use:

- Cards.
- Icons.
- Tags.
- Progress indicators.
- Interactive elements.
- Any other suitable representation.

The design should remain readable and consistent with the overall portfolio.

Only include technologies that you are genuinely familiar with.

---

# Task 3: The Archives

### Projects & Work — 30 points

The most important part of the archive is the work you have created.

Showcase at least **2 projects**.

### Subtask 3.1 — Project Records (20 pts)

Each project should contain:

- Project name.
- Short description.
- Technologies used.
- Relevant image / screenshot / visual.
- Link to the project or repository, where applicable.

A visitor should be able to understand what the project does without having to inspect the source code.

### Subtask 3.2 — Project Presentation (10 pts)

Present your projects as part of one cohesive portfolio experience.

You may use:

- Project cards.
- Interactive panels.
- Timeline layouts.
- Expandable sections.
- Terminal-style interfaces.
- Command-centre interfaces.
- Any other creative presentation.

The focus should be on making the projects easy to understand and visually engaging.

---

# Task 4: The Doomsday Interface

### Design & User Experience — 15 points

The portfolio should clearly reflect the **Doomsday** theme.

The theme is open to interpretation.

You could imagine your portfolio as:

- A survivor's terminal.
- A futuristic control centre.
- A post-apocalyptic archive.
- A system monitoring dashboard.
- A classified digital record.
- A final transmission from the surviving network.

### Subtask 4.1 — The Interface (10 pts)

Create a consistent visual identity across the website.

The interface should include:

- Clear typography and hierarchy.
- Consistent spacing and alignment.
- Responsive design.
- Smooth navigation.
- Appropriate animations or interactions.
- Readable content.

The Doomsday theme should influence **how the portfolio feels**, not simply appear as a background image.

### Subtask 4.2 — Responsive Experience (5 pts)

The portfolio should remain usable across different screen sizes.

Ensure that:

- Content does not overflow on smaller screens.
- Navigation remains accessible.
- Projects and skill sections adapt appropriately.
- Text remains readable.
- Interactive elements remain usable.

---

# Task 5: The Final Transmission

### Contact & Deployment — 10 points

The archive is useless if nobody can access it.

### Subtask 5.1 — Contact & Links (5 pts)

Provide a clear way for visitors to connect with you.

Include relevant links such as:

- GitHub.
- LinkedIn.
- Email.
- Other professional links.

You may present these however you like.

### Subtask 5.2 — Deployment (5 pts)

Deploy your portfolio so that it can be accessed through a public URL.

Suitable platforms include:

- GitHub Pages.
- Vercel.
- Netlify.
- Other equivalent hosting platforms.

The submitted link should open the completed portfolio directly.

---

# Final Mission

By the end of the task, your portfolio should allow a visitor to:

- Understand who you are.
- Learn about your background and interests.
- Explore your technical skills.
- View at least **2 projects**.
- Understand what each project does.
- Access your relevant external links.
- Navigate the website comfortably.
- Access the portfolio through a public URL.
- Experience a clear **Doomsday-themed** visual identity.

The goal is to build a **personal portfolio**, not simply a webpage containing your resume.

Your portfolio should feel like **your digital identity**.

## Theme

The entire portfolio should be designed around the **Doomsday / Last Survivor / Digital Archive** setting.

You are free to interpret the visual style creatively, but the theme should be visible in both the interface and the way the portfolio presents information.

The website does not need to look like a literal destroyed world.

The theme can instead be expressed through:

- Futuristic interfaces.
- System status displays.
- Terminal elements.
- Glitch effects.
- Emergency alerts.
- Digital archives.
- HUD-style components.
- Creative animations.
- Story-driven navigation.

The final result should still feel like a **professional personal portfolio**.
